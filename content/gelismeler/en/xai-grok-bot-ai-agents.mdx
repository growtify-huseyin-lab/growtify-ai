xAI (branded on the page as SpaceXAI) introduced a product it calls Grok Bot: AI "teammates" with their own computer in the cloud that sign into the tools you use and work 24/7. The idea: instead of prompting an assistant, you hand off work the way you would to a colleague.

## In brief

- **What was announced:** Grok Bot, on August 11. The agents have their own computer in the cloud; they sign into the apps you use (inbox, CRM, web tools) and run jobs end to end, coming back only when they need your approval. They're said to work even on platforms with no clean API.
- **How it works:** you hand off work by messaging a bot from your phone or desktop, like texting a coworker. Multiple bots can run in parallel (one managing the others), and bots message each other and coordinate in group chats. Show a bot a task once and it saves it as a "routine" to run itself next time.
- **Access:** it's in beta, available on desktop and iOS to SuperGrok Heavy, Cursor Ultra, and Cursor Teams Premium subscribers. Enterprise users can join a waitlist. No underlying model, benchmark, or pricing details were shared.
- **Honesty note:** all the examples and glowing quotes on the page are from xAI's own team (sales, ops, product). So they're the company's own account, not independent testing — lines like "I just trust it to run forever without verifying" should be read in that light.

## Our take

Unlike a developer tool, this aims right at our audience's world: agents doing sales, marketing, and operations work inside your actual tools. If it works as described, it's the kind of leverage a small team really feels — and it's where "agentic AI" is heading. Two honest cautions. First, everything here is xAI's own telling; there's no independent testing, no benchmarks, and access is narrow (specific paid tiers). So treat it as a beta to watch, not a proven tool. Second — and this is the part to take seriously — an agent that signs into your inbox and CRM and acts on its own is powerful precisely because it can do real things, and that same capability is why safety bodies have [been warning about autonomous agents](/en/news/uk-aisi-cyber-testing-agent-incident) lately. The page's "trust it to run forever without reviewing" framing is exactly what you should not do on day one. The right move: start with low-stakes tasks, keep a human in the loop, and grow the trust as the agent earns it.
