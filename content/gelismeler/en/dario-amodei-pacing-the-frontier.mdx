Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei published an opinion essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier." His core idea: we should deliberately slow the pace at which we improve AI capabilities — not to halt it, but so safety can keep pace. "Progress will still seem fast," he writes.

## In brief

- **Main thesis:** "We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." Amodei frames this as keeping safety in step with capability, not halting.
- **The reasoning:** AI systems building their own next generations (recursive self-improvement) is driving the acceleration, and alignment (keeping a model on-purpose) techniques aren't keeping up. The essay points to a recent AI-cyber incident (OpenAI-Hugging Face) as an example.
- **His three steps:** (1) a unilateral Anthropic commitment — independent "embedded evaluators" with employee-like access who can publish findings uncensored (bar security/legal redactions); (2) common safety standards among frontier labs (with government mediation); (3) global coordination (four escalating levels, from a bioweapons ban to a full pause).
- **What it is, and its limits:** this is an opinion/advocacy essay, not a product. Its numbers are Amodei's own predictions: cures for most major diseases in 5-10 years, misaligned systems potentially taking over the internet in 6-12 months, a 3-5 year geopolitical window. Some proposals (like chip export restrictions) are contested policy positions.

## Our take

Let's be upfront: this is an opinion essay from a frontier-lab CEO — an influential but single voice in a contested field, not settled fact, and his own company has a stake in how the resulting rules get drawn. Still, one part stands out: a company proposing to deliberately slow itself mid-race and invite outside evaluators who can publish findings uncensored is unusual. For our audience there's nothing to act on today; it's a vision/governance piece. But the direction matters: "how fast is too fast, and who checks" is moving from abstract debate to concrete proposals. Read his predictions (disease cures, botnet timelines) as his estimates, not proven facts, and note the geopolitical proposals are contested. The concrete examples of the risks he describes show up the same week in [Anthropic's own threat report](/en/news/anthropic-threat-report-september-2026).
