The US is turning AI into a national science strategy. Energy Secretary Chris Wright announced the first projects under Genesis Mission, a program aiming to "double the nation's scientific productivity": 278 projects were selected from all 50 states, with 342 institutions taking part. The department calls it the largest funding response in its history.

## In brief

- **What was announced:** the first 278 Genesis Mission projects were selected — 168 led by universities, 87 by national labs, 19 by companies, 4 by nonprofits.
- **The goal and the tools:** combine the DOE's science infrastructure, AI, and supercomputers to speed up discovery. Teams get access to a "Genesis Mission Platform" with AI agent frameworks, industry AI models, and national-lab compute.
- **Where it's aimed:** nuclear energy, commercial fusion, intelligent chip design, and critical-mineral extraction. The largest project is a 3-year, $60 million investment using AI to speed up nuclear-facility delivery.
- **The number:** $293 million in support was announced for the program. (Note: selection isn't a firm commitment until negotiations conclude.)

## Our take

This isn't a tool you'll use today; it's a government program, and the results will play out over years. But the direction matters: it's one of the most concrete links in the "AI is now strategic technology" line we've been tracking for weeks. When a country declares "accelerating scientific discovery" in chips and energy an official goal and gathers hundreds of projects under one roof, this stops being a product race between companies and becomes states building AI as core infrastructure. For a small business, the lesson is indirect but real: as AI gets cheaper and governments invest at this scale, "AI-accelerated" competitors will become normal in your sector too. There's nothing to rush today — but it's a good sign of which way the wind is blowing.
