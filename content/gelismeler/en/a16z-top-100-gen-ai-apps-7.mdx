Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) published the seventh edition of its ranking of the 100 most-used consumer gen AI apps. The list draws on web traffic, mobile users, and spending data; the data is for US consumers, mostly from August 2026.

## In brief

- **The top:** on web, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and DeepSeek; on mobile, ChatGPT, CapCut, Canva, Character.ai, and Gemini. In US consumer spending, the top three are ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Only 11 new products entered this edition, the fewest across the report's seven editions.
- **Wide but shallow use:** according to the report, about half of US consumers say they use AI, but only 25% use it daily. In August, 4.5% of consumers had a paid subscription to ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, up from 2.1% a year earlier.
- **Concentrated spending:** the top 1% of spenders account for roughly half of observed spending. The median AI payer spends $25 a month. Heavy spenders mostly pay for coding, productivity, and creative tools.
- **Personal agents and platform fights:** the report sees personal AI agents as a rising theme. It notes Amazon [shut down Meta's Muse agent](/en/news/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-agentic-shopping) in under two weeks, while companies like Shopify, Instacart, and Expedia signed official integrations. It also writes that Claude rose quickly for a while and then saw its average daily desktop sessions decline in July and August, while ChatGPT re-accelerated.

## Our take

To us, the report's most useful line is this: AI use is wide, but not very deep for almost anyone. Most people use AI occasionally and lightly; most of the spending, and likely most of the value, sits with a small group who've built it into their daily work. That group mostly pays for coding, productivity, and creative work. For anyone trying to grow a business with AI, the message is clear: the difference comes less from which tool you use than from how deeply you build AI into your work. Another note in the report is about niche focus: products like OpenEvidence, which serves only physicians, can make the list. Going deep on a specific profession or sector may be a more realistic path than competing with the general-purpose giants. Two caveats: the data is US-only, and the ranking is based on traffic, which isn't always the same as value.
