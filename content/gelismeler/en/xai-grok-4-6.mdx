xAI (branded on the page as SpaceXAI) released its new model, Grok 4.6. The focus is long-running agents and more ambitious visual/interactive work, and it's said to be better at testing and verifying its own output.

## In brief

- **What shipped:** Grok 4.6, on August 12. It improves clearly over the previous 4.5 across every shared benchmark. xAI says it's especially strong at "turning a broad product idea into a working first version."
- **Price:** $2 input / $6 output per million tokens (the fast variant costs twice as much). That's cheap for a model in this class.
- **Comparison (xAI's own measurements):** a neck-and-neck picture against GPT-5.6 Sol and Fable 5 — ahead on some (e.g., GDPVal-AA, AA-Briefcase, Harvey LAB, and above GPT on CursorBench), behind on others (below both on Terminal-Bench 3.0 and DeepSWE). So it's competitive with the frontier, but not "first at everything."
- **Access:** through Cursor and Grok Build, via the xAI API, and through partners like OpenRouter, Vercel, and Cloudflare. Grok Build and Cursor include 2x usage for the first week.

## Our take

Two things are true at once here. One is clear: the model is a real generational jump over its predecessor — it beats 4.5 on every shared benchmark. The other is more mixed: against frontier models (GPT-5.6, Fable 5) it's ahead on some tests and behind on others, notably trailing both on Terminal-Bench and DeepSWE. For our audience, the part that stands out is price: $2/$6 per million tokens is cheap for this class, and the model is widely available (Cursor, OpenRouter, Vercel, Cloudflare). So if you do coding, agent, or app-building work, it's a real, low-cost option worth trying. Two honest caveats: the numbers are xAI's own tests, and independent comparisons take weeks; and "competitive" isn't "best" — it's clearly behind on some coding benchmarks. In short: strong on price and access, but wait for independent tests before any firm verdict.
